LEAD: Indian Railways has quietly delivered one of the most operationally consequential logistics achievements in the country's post-Independence history. Freight loading has grown from 1,098 million tonnes in 2014-15 to 1,670 million tonnes in 2025-26 — a 52 per cent expansion across eleven years, and a fresh all-time record for annual freight carried. The 2025-26 figure represents a 3.25 per cent increase over the previous year's 1,617 million tonnes, and it positions Indian Railways as the second-largest freight-carrying railway system in the world. Every tonne of this expansion strengthens the operational architecture through which India's farms, industries, markets and ports are progressively connected into an efficient, integrated logistics network.

## What drove the 52 per cent surge

Three institutional interventions have progressively delivered the freight capacity expansion. The Dedicated Freight Corridors (DFCs) — the 1,337 km Eastern DFC (Ludhiana-Sonnagar) and the 1,506 km Western DFC (Dadri-Jawaharlal Nehru Port) — together now carry an average of 443 freight trains per day, separating heavy freight from mixed passenger corridors and giving industrial and port axes reliable rail access. A new DFC connecting Dankuni (East) to Surat (West) was announced in the Union Budget 2026, with its Detailed Project Report currently under preparation.

The Gati Shakti Multi-Modal Cargo Terminal (GCT) policy has progressively commissioned 142 terminals as of August 2026 — with a combined capacity of 224 million tonnes per annum, mobilising approximately Rs 10,000 crore of private investment. In FY 2025-26 alone, GCTs handled 146 million tonnes of freight.

The full electrification of the broad-gauge network, the deployment of double-stack container services, long-haul freight trains carrying up to 360 TEUs, and progressive expansion of industry-owned rolling stock (275 special-purpose rakes and 397 general-purpose rakes now operational) together complete the operational picture.

## What it means

For India's logistics economy, the 52 per cent freight expansion progressively shifts more cargo from congested highways to reliable, low-cost rail — reducing per-tonne logistics costs by approximately 45 per cent compared with road transport, and taking one freight train's worth of cargo off the highway for every 300-plus trucks displaced. For India's climate architecture, the fully electrified broad-gauge network progressively delivers freight movement with substantially lower emissions than road alternatives. For India's industrial base, the reliable rail-freight backbone connects steel mills to iron ore mines, power plants to coalfields, cement factories to construction demand, and fertiliser plants to agricultural markets. For India's global standing, the second-place ranking behind only China represents one of the most consequential single achievements in the country's post-2014 infrastructure trajectory.

From 1,098 million tonnes to 1,670 million tonnes. From marginal to world-second. From fragmented network to integrated logistics architecture. Indian Railways has quietly become one of the operational engines of Aatmanirbhar Bharat — moving the goods that build the country.
