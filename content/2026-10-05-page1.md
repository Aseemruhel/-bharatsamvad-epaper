LEAD: India has emerged as one of the deepest aerospace industrial partners European aerospace companies possess anywhere outside the European Union itself. A combination of the January 2026 India-EU Free Trade Agreement, substantial European aerospace investments now operationally live across three Indian states, and the progressive scaling of joint production and maintenance architecture has together transformed India from a conventional aerospace customer into a joint-production partner embedded within European aerospace supply chains. The transformation covers civil aviation engine maintenance, military engine servicing, military transport aircraft assembly, and helicopter manufacturing — four distinct dimensions of the aerospace industrial stack, now all progressively present within Indian borders.

## What is now live inside India

Four substantive aerospace industrial facilities have been progressively activated across India as part of this transformation.

A $232 million engine Maintenance, Repair and Overhaul (MRO) facility is now operational in Hyderabad, dedicated to the CFM Leap engine family that powers India's civil aviation narrowbody fleet. The facility is designed to overhaul 300 engines annually, scaling toward a workforce of 1,100 Indian specialists at full operational capacity. It is the largest facility of its kind globally for the engine family it services.

An adjacent $46 million military engine servicing shop will overhaul over 600 M88 engine modules annually — the engines powering the Rafale combat aircraft fleet that India has progressively inducted into the Indian Air Force. This represents the first facility of its kind for these engines anywhere outside France, delivering strategic sovereignty over the servicing of one of the most consequential single combat platforms in India's current defence inventory.

A military transport aircraft assembly line is progressively ramping up in Vadodara, Gujarat, under the Airbus-Tata partnership. Of the 56 C-295 transport aircraft on order for the Indian Air Force, 40 are being assembled on Indian soil — the first time a complete European military transport aircraft line has been established outside the EU.

A helicopter production line is being commissioned in Karnataka to manufacture the H125 light utility helicopter, with the Karnataka output progressively planned for export markets as well as domestic deployment.

## The January 2026 India-EU FTA — the enabling layer

Underlying every element of this industrial transformation is the India-EU Free Trade Agreement that entered force in January 2026. The FTA progressively eliminates tariffs on nearly all aircraft and spacecraft components traded between India and the European Union — a substantive change for an industry in which complex multi-country supply chains and expensive certified components are the operational norm.

The commercial scale of what the FTA liberalises is substantial. CFM Leap engines currently power approximately 80 per cent of India's narrowbody passenger aircraft fleet. More than 2,000 additional Leap engines are on order from Indian airlines as the country's commercial aviation sector progressively expands. Tariff elimination on this scale of recurring component trade progressively reduces costs for Indian airlines, improves the economics of Indian-based engine maintenance, and strengthens the operational case for every subsequent aerospace industrial investment in the country.

## Why this is not just outsourcing

For most of India's post-Independence industrial history, aerospace engagement with Western suppliers followed a conventional pattern — India procured aircraft, engines or components from external manufacturers; the manufacturing happened abroad; India paid for the output and operated it. Industrial depth and strategic sovereignty remained located in the supplier country.

The current India-Europe aerospace architecture breaks that pattern substantively. The engines being overhauled in Hyderabad are being overhauled by Indian specialists on Indian soil, using Indian industrial infrastructure. The Rafale engine modules being serviced in India are modules that previously would have been shipped back to France. The C-295 transport aircraft being assembled in Gujarat are being assembled by Indian workers using Indian industrial facilities. The H125 helicopters being produced in Karnataka include components manufactured in Indian supply chains. Across each dimension, operational capability that previously sat exclusively in Europe is progressively being replicated — permanently — within Indian industry.

That is a substantively different industrial relationship from outsourcing. Outsourcing is contingent — it exists only while the commercial terms suit both sides, and can be withdrawn when circumstances change. Joint production, shared MRO, and co-located industrial depth are substantially harder to unwind. Once Indian specialists hold the technical knowledge, once Indian supply chains have been certified, once Indian facilities have been accredited by European and global aviation regulators, that industrial base progressively becomes part of the global aerospace architecture rather than a dependent extension of it.

## Why it matters for India

For India's civil aviation sector, the Hyderabad MRO facility progressively keeps substantial engine-maintenance revenue inside the country, reduces downtime for Indian airline fleets, and progressively builds a global-tier aviation workforce rooted on Indian soil. For India's defence architecture, in-country Rafale engine servicing progressively eliminates the operational dependence that previously required Indian military engines to be shipped overseas for overhaul — a substantive strategic sovereignty gain for one of the Indian Air Force's most consequential combat platforms. For India's Make in India framework, the Vadodara C-295 line and the Karnataka H125 line progressively demonstrate that complete military aircraft can be manufactured within Indian industry — a milestone that no previous phase of Indian aerospace policy had achieved at this scale. And for India's industrial workforce, the 1,100 specialists at Hyderabad and the thousands of further workers across Vadodara, Karnataka and the wider supplier ecosystem represent one of the most substantive single aerospace employment expansions India has recorded.

For India's strategic positioning in global aerospace, the broader significance is substantive. India has progressively joined the small group of nations that possess full-stack aerospace industrial capability — civil engine MRO, military engine servicing, military transport assembly, helicopter production — all within national borders. That capacity, once established, is substantially durable.

## What the FTA progressively unlocks

The January 2026 India-EU FTA is the operational foundation on which every subsequent industrial investment rests. Tariff elimination on aircraft and spacecraft components progressively changes the economics of every aerospace supply-chain decision made by European manufacturers evaluating India as a production base. Components can move freely between EU manufacturing sites and Indian industrial facilities without tariff friction. Spare parts can be stocked in Indian warehouses for both domestic and regional markets. Indian-manufactured components can progressively flow back into European production lines, embedding Indian suppliers within European aerospace supply chains at every tier.

Over the coming decade, the cumulative effect of this tariff elimination — combined with the industrial infrastructure now being built on the ground — is progressively likely to make India one of the primary non-EU manufacturing bases for European aerospace, both civil and military.

## What it signals

For India's strategic sovereignty, the shift from engine import dependence to indigenous MRO progressively eliminates one of the historical vulnerabilities of Indian defence and civil aviation. For India's industrial transformation, the four aerospace facilities now operational or being commissioned represent one of the most consequential single foreign-investment-backed industrial expansions India has delivered this decade. For India's position in the global aerospace supply chain, the Hyderabad MRO, Vadodara assembly line, and Karnataka helicopter facility progressively establish India as a global production base rather than a destination market. And for India-EU bilateral relations, the aerospace architecture progressively anchors one of the most substantive industrial partnerships either side possesses — one that extends far beyond trade statistics into shared industrial infrastructure, shared workforce development, and shared technological depth.

From customer to co-producer. From engine importer to engine overhauler. From platform operator to platform assembler. From tariff-exposed to FTA-protected. India's aerospace industrial transformation, enabled by the India-EU FTA and progressively activated through on-ground industrial investments, captures one of the most substantive single phases of Indian industrial deepening currently underway in any sector of the economy.
