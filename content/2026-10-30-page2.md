LEAD: The 19th Session of the India–Belgium–Luxembourg Economic Union (BLEU) Joint Economic Commission (JEC) has been held in Luxembourg, providing a formal institutional platform for India, Belgium and Luxembourg to review bilateral trade and investment relations and to discuss opportunities to further strengthen economic cooperation across the key sectors that anchor the India-BLEU commercial partnership. The discussions covered a broad range of priority sectors — logistics with a focus on air connectivity and ports, renewable energy and clean technology, financial services and FinTech, digital transformation and information and communication technology, healthcare and life sciences, Ayurveda and traditional medicine, and the agro and food processing industry. The 19th JEC extends one of the most consistent bilateral institutional dialogues India maintains with any European partner — the BLEU customs union between Belgium and Luxembourg has existed since 1921, and the India-BLEU JEC mechanism has operationally sustained a continuous commercial conversation across almost two decades of its own institutional history. The eight-sector agenda discussed in Luxembourg captures the depth and breadth of the India-BLEU commercial relationship as it stands today.

## Why the BLEU partnership matters for India

The Belgium–Luxembourg Economic Union is one of the oldest continuously functioning customs unions in modern economic history, originally established in 1921 and progressively expanded into a wider framework of trade and monetary cooperation that eventually anchored both countries' integration into the European Union. For India, the BLEU bloc operationally represents two of the European Union's most consequential small-but-strategic economies — Belgium as a major European trade and logistics hub anchored on the Port of Antwerp, and Luxembourg as one of the world's most consequential financial services, investment fund and FinTech centres.

Belgium is one of India's largest trading partners in the European Union, with bilateral trade historically anchored by the Antwerp–Surat diamond corridor — one of the oldest commercial relationships between any two cities on Earth, moving a large share of the world's cut and polished diamonds between Indian artisans and the Antwerp diamond-trading complex. Beyond diamonds, Belgium's role as a European gateway port, pharmaceutical hub, chemicals producer, and advanced-manufacturing centre makes it operationally one of India's most commercially consequential European partners.

Luxembourg, despite its small geographic size, is operationally one of the most important financial jurisdictions globally. It hosts the second-largest investment-fund centre in the world after the United States, is the European Union's leading centre for green finance and sustainable investment, and has progressively emerged as one of the leading FinTech jurisdictions in continental Europe. For India's financial services and FinTech sector, Luxembourg represents one of the most strategically positioned European partners — one that combines access to European capital markets with regulatory experience in both traditional fund management and emerging digital-finance architecture.

## The eight-sector agenda — what the Luxembourg talks covered

The 19th JEC's agenda spanned the full operational range of the India-BLEU commercial relationship. Each of the eight priority sectors discussed carries its own strategic logic for the three-country partnership.

Logistics, with a focus on air connectivity and ports — Belgium's Port of Antwerp-Bruges is one of Europe's largest and most sophisticated container and chemical ports, and the air connectivity dimension reflects both the direct Brussels-India air corridor and the broader cargo and passenger movement that anchors tourism, business travel, and freight exchange between India and the Benelux region. Expanded shipping cooperation, port-to-port logistics partnerships, and improved air connectivity between Indian metros and the BLEU hubs are the operational outcomes being pursued.

Renewable energy and clean technology — Belgium and Luxembourg are both leaders in European decarbonisation, offshore wind development, green hydrogen research, and clean-technology commercialisation. For India, which is scaling renewable capacity at one of the fastest rates in the world and is driving the International Solar Alliance and the Global Biofuels Alliance, cooperation with BLEU partners offers both technology-transfer potential and co-investment opportunities in Indian renewable projects.

Financial services and FinTech — Luxembourg's position as the European Union's leading investment-fund jurisdiction and as a progressive FinTech hub operationally positions it as a natural partner for India's rapidly scaling financial-services and FinTech sector. India's own FinTech ecosystem — anchored by UPI, Jan Dhan accounts, Aadhaar-enabled payments, and now the Digital Rupee pilot — is one of the most consequential digital-finance infrastructures globally. Luxembourg's regulatory expertise and India's deployment scale create a natural complementarity.

Digital transformation and ICT — India's position as one of the world's leading ICT services exporters, combined with the BLEU's enterprise-digitalisation requirements and the European Union's broader digital-sovereignty agenda, makes digital transformation one of the most commercially active dimensions of the India-BLEU relationship. Indian IT services companies have significant operations across both Belgium and Luxembourg.

Healthcare and life sciences — Belgium is one of Europe's major pharmaceutical centres, hosting significant operations of global pharmaceutical companies and a strong biotechnology research ecosystem. India is the world's largest supplier of generic medicines and vaccines — the pharmacy of the world — and bilateral cooperation in life sciences spans vaccine development, pharmaceutical manufacturing, clinical research, and biotechnology commercialisation.

Ayurveda and traditional medicine — the inclusion of Ayurveda and traditional medicine as a formal priority sector reflects both India's progressive international promotion of its traditional medicine systems under the Ministry of AYUSH framework and the growing European interest in holistic, preventive and natural wellness approaches. The World Health Organization's Global Centre for Traditional Medicine, hosted by India at Jamnagar, anchors a broader international mainstreaming agenda for Ayurveda that cooperation with BLEU partners can operationally advance.

Agro and food processing — India's status as one of the world's largest producers of agricultural commodities, combined with Belgium's and Luxembourg's advanced food-processing industry, cold-chain infrastructure, and European market access, creates significant commercial potential in processed food, specialty food, cold-chain logistics, and agricultural technology.

## The institutional continuity story

Nineteen continuous sessions of a formal bilateral economic commission mechanism represent, in themselves, a diplomatic achievement that deserves attention. Few bilateral institutional dialogues India maintains with any European Union member state carry comparable continuity. The BLEU JEC mechanism has operationally sustained a continuous commercial conversation across changes of government in India, in Belgium, and in Luxembourg; across the global financial crisis, the eurozone crisis, Brexit, COVID-19, and the Russia-Ukraine war; and across the evolution of India's own commercial profile from a predominantly services-exporting economy toward a progressively diversified manufacturing-plus-services economy under the Aatmanirbhar Bharat framework.

That continuity is operationally valuable. It means Indian and BLEU businesses operate within a predictable institutional framework. It means commercial disputes have a formal review mechanism. It means new sectoral priorities — Ayurveda, FinTech, clean technology — can be progressively added to the agenda as the relationship evolves. And it means the broader India-European Union architecture, including the ongoing India-EU Free Trade Agreement negotiations, the India-EU Trade and Technology Council, and the India-EU Connectivity Partnership, operates against a backdrop of consistent bilateral commercial dialogue with one of the EU's most strategically positioned small-economy groupings.

## Where the opportunities now lie

The 19th JEC's eight-sector agenda operationally captures where the India-BLEU commercial opportunities now lie. In logistics, deeper port cooperation, expanded air connectivity, and multimodal freight corridors between Indian metros and the Benelux hubs offer immediate commercial potential. In renewable energy and clean technology, co-investment in Indian renewable projects, technology partnerships in offshore wind and green hydrogen, and joint commercialisation of clean-tech innovation represent one of the fastest-growing dimensions of the relationship. In financial services and FinTech, Luxembourg's investment-fund expertise and India's digital-finance infrastructure create natural complementarity for cross-border fund structures, FinTech partnerships, and sustainable-finance instruments targeting Indian markets.

In digital transformation and ICT, continued expansion of Indian IT services' presence in the Benelux region, enterprise-digitalisation partnerships, and cooperation on emerging technologies including artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, and digital-sovereignty architecture represent the services-sector core of the relationship. In healthcare and life sciences, deeper vaccine and pharmaceutical cooperation, clinical research partnerships, and biotechnology commercialisation extend the pandemic-era cooperation that validated India's role as a global pharmaceutical supplier. In Ayurveda and traditional medicine, the formal inclusion as a JEC priority sector operationally advances the broader international mainstreaming agenda India is pursuing through the WHO Global Centre for Traditional Medicine at Jamnagar. In agro and food processing, cold-chain partnerships, processed-food exports, and agricultural-technology cooperation extend India's agricultural value chain into high-value European markets.

## What it signals

For India's European strategy, the 19th JEC operationally confirms the depth and continuity of one of India's most consequential small-economy partnerships in the European Union. The BLEU partnership complements the broader India-EU architecture by providing a specific, long-standing institutional platform with two European economies that occupy strategic niches — Belgian port and logistics strength, Luxembourg financial-services depth — that no other EU partner duplicates. For Belgian and Luxembourgish businesses, the 19th JEC operationally signals formal government endorsement of the eight-sector commercial agenda, providing the institutional framework within which business delegations, trade missions, and investment-facilitation initiatives can advance over the coming period.

For India's broader commercial profile, the formal inclusion of Ayurveda and traditional medicine as a JEC priority sector operationally advances India's international promotion of its traditional-medicine systems. For India's financial-services sector, the Luxembourg engagement operationally extends one of the most strategically positioned European platforms for cross-border fund structures and FinTech partnerships. For India's clean-technology trajectory, cooperation with BLEU partners anchored in European green-finance expertise and clean-technology commercialisation operationally supports India's renewable scale-up and climate commitments.

From the 1921 origins of the Belgium-Luxembourg Economic Union to the nineteenth continuous session of the India-BLEU JEC in Luxembourg. From the Antwerp-Surat diamond corridor to Luxembourg's investment-fund jurisdiction serving Indian capital-market partnerships. From traditional sectors of trade to the new frontier sectors — FinTech, clean technology, Ayurveda, agro and food processing — that now anchor the India-BLEU commercial agenda. The 19th India-BLEU JEC in Luxembourg captures what consistent institutional dialogue, long-horizon commercial relationships, and eight-sector operational depth look like in practice. It is one of the most consistent and consequential bilateral economic mechanisms India maintains anywhere in the European Union.
