LEAD: One of the most quietly consequential milestones in India's northeast energy-infrastructure trajectory took place on Tuesday, 18 August 2026, at Dimapur — the largest urban centre of Nagaland and the state's principal industrial and commercial hub. Indradhanush Gas Grid Limited (IGGL), the five-CPSE joint venture that has been building the North East Gas Grid since 2018, successfully injected natural gas into the newly-commissioned Dergaon-Dimapur Pipeline. In that single technical event, Nagaland became the first of India's eight northeastern states to be integrated into the National Gas Grid via the emerging northeast pipeline architecture. For a state whose energy consumers — households, small industries, commercial enterprises, transport operators — have historically relied on liquefied petroleum gas, diesel, kerosene and other higher-emissions fuels for the vast majority of thermal energy requirements, the arrival of piped natural gas represents a structural shift in the operational energy environment.

The arrival is consequential on two independent dimensions. First, it substantially improves Nagaland's energy access — cleaner, reliable, priced-competitively natural gas now becomes available to industrial, commercial and eventually residential consumers in the state. Second, and equally significantly, the same infrastructure enables the evacuation and monetisation of natural gas produced from ONGC's Jorhat Asset in Assam — gas that had previously been under-utilised or flared due to the absence of downstream pipeline connectivity. Simultaneously with the Dergaon-Dimapur Pipeline commissioning, ONGC brought online gas evacuation facilities at Khoraghat GGS-1 in Assam's Golaghat district, enabling approximately 1.10 lakh Standard Cubic Metres per day of associated natural gas from the Upper Assam Shelf to flow through the NEGG rather than being wasted. Union Minister for Petroleum and Natural Gas Hardeep Singh Puri, describing the development on X on 17 August, said the pipeline "will help reduce gas flaring, substitute equivalent LNG imports and unlock hundreds of crores of value annually" — an estimated ₹300 crore in annual economic value.

## What the technical architecture actually does

The 18 August commissioning brings together three interlocking infrastructure elements that together transform northeast gas connectivity.

Element one is the Dergaon-Dimapur Pipeline itself — the new natural gas pipeline connecting Dergaon in Assam's Golaghat district to Dimapur in Nagaland. This is one of the substantial branch pipelines of the wider North East Gas Grid, extending piped gas connectivity into Nagaland for the first time. Until 18 August, Nagaland had no natural gas pipeline connectivity of any kind. Now, gas flows through the pipeline into Dimapur and is progressively available for city gas distribution, industrial supply, and eventual last-mile residential connections.

Element two is the Khoraghat GGS-1 evacuation facility. GGS-1 stands for Gas Gathering Station 1 — the upstream facility in Assam that collects associated natural gas produced alongside crude oil from the Upper Assam Shelf. Until Tuesday's commissioning, gas produced at Khoraghat was substantially under-utilised or flared (burned off unproductively at the well-head) due to the absence of pipeline connectivity to any downstream market. The new evacuation facility now processes this gas and feeds it into the NEGG for onward transmission.

Element three is ONGC's Jorhat Asset — the upstream production field whose natural gas now becomes commercially monetisable rather than being wasted. Approximately 1.10 lakh SCM per day of Jorhat Asset gas will now flow through the pipeline architecture to reach downstream customers, principally in Nagaland but with wider northeast distribution potential as the NEGG progressively expands.

Together, the three elements convert what had been a stranded upstream resource (Jorhat gas without evacuation capacity) and an under-served downstream market (Nagaland without gas connectivity) into a unified commercial operation. Every SCM of gas produced at Jorhat now has a market. Every consumer in Nagaland now has access to a cleaner, cheaper alternative to imported LNG and higher-emissions fuels.

## The economic and environmental arithmetic

The immediate operational impact of the 18 August commissioning is substantial across multiple dimensions.

Volume: approximately 1.10 lakh Standard Cubic Metres per day of Jorhat Asset gas monetised. Over a year, this aggregates to roughly 40 million SCM — a substantial quantity that would otherwise have been flared or under-utilised.

Import substitution: the 1.10 lakh SCM daily volume directly reduces LNG imports by an equivalent quantity. India imports substantial volumes of LNG from Qatar, the United States, Australia and other suppliers to meet its natural gas demand; every SCM of domestically-produced gas that reaches domestic markets is one SCM less that needs to be imported. At current LNG prices, this substitution generates real foreign-exchange savings.

Economic value: Union Minister Puri's estimate of "hundreds of crores of rupees annually" translates, per industry sources briefed on the project, to approximately ₹300 crore per year in aggregate economic value — combining the direct sales revenue on the gas, the import-substitution value, and the wider industrial-activity multiplier effects.

Emissions reduction: gas that would previously have been flared (with all associated carbon and methane emissions) is now productively utilised. This alone represents one of the most substantial single environmental improvements in northeast hydrocarbon operations in recent years.

Consumer access: Nagaland households, industries, commercial enterprises and transport operators now progressively gain access to natural gas as an alternative to LPG, diesel, kerosene and other fuels — with associated cost, cleanliness, and reliability advantages.

## The Indradhanush Gas Grid — building the northeast connection

The organisational architecture that has delivered this 18 August milestone is one of the most substantial single-purpose institutional creations of India's post-2016 energy trajectory. Indradhanush Gas Grid Limited (IGGL) was incorporated on 10 August 2018 as a joint venture of five Central Public Sector Enterprises — Indian Oil Corporation, ONGC, GAIL (India), Oil India Limited, and Numaligarh Refinery Limited. Its exclusive mandate: implement the North East Gas Grid pipeline architecture envisaged under the Hydrocarbon Vision 2030 for North-East India that the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas released in 2016.

The NEGG is a substantial engineering undertaking. Total pipeline length: 1,656 kilometres, spanning eight states with substantially challenging terrain across mountains, rivers, forest reserves, and geologically active zones. Estimated cost: ₹9,265 crore, with the Union Cabinet approving a 60 per cent viability gap funding grant to make the project commercially executable. The full grid, when complete, will connect Guwahati (the northeast's principal urban centre) to Itanagar, Dimapur, Kohima, Imphal, Aizawl, Agartala, Shillong, Silchar, Gangtok, and Numaligarh — every capital city and major demand centre of the northeast region.

Along the way, IGGL has produced substantial engineering achievements that few observers outside the sector have registered. In 2023, Ace Energy Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd. completed one of the world's largest single Horizontal Directional Drilling (HDD) crossings — a 3,600-metre pipeline installation across the Subansiri River in Assam — as part of the IGGL programme. The Sikkim segment groundbreaking took place at Pakyong in July 2023. Progressive commissionings across Assam and now Nagaland have brought the network closer to full operational status. Substantial additional connectivity into Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya and Arunachal Pradesh remains progressively under construction.

The 18 August commissioning is not the completion of the NEGG. It is one substantial milestone along the trajectory. But it is the first time a state that was previously entirely outside the national gas architecture — Nagaland — has been formally integrated. And it is the first time that associated gas from ONGC's Jorhat production has had substantial downstream evacuation capacity to enable full commercial monetisation.

## Why Nagaland matters — and why now

Nagaland's inclusion in the national gas architecture carries substantial significance for the state's economic and developmental trajectory.

Historically, Nagaland has been one of India's most substantially energy-constrained states. Grid electricity supply has been progressively strengthening but remains substantially below national averages in reliability and coverage. Piped cooking gas (PNG) has been essentially non-existent for domestic consumers. Industrial development has been constrained by fuel-supply reliability and cost. Transport has depended predominantly on diesel and petrol. The cumulative effect is that Nagaland's per-capita energy consumption is substantially below the national average, and industrial establishment costs are substantially higher than they would be with reliable natural gas access.

The Dergaon-Dimapur Pipeline commissioning progressively begins to change this substantially. Industrial users in Dimapur — the state's principal commercial and industrial hub — can now design new plant investments around natural gas availability. Existing industries can substitute higher-cost diesel and LPG with cheaper piped gas. Progressive expansion of city gas distribution networks in Dimapur (and eventually Kohima and other Nagaland cities) will bring PNG to residential kitchens. CNG for transport becomes progressively available.

For a state that has substantially been at the margins of India's post-1991 industrial expansion, the arrival of piped natural gas represents one of the most consequential structural improvements in its operational business environment that any single infrastructure development can provide. This is the substantial promise that the Hydrocarbon Vision 2030 framework carries — and 18 August 2026 is one of the first days on which that promise has been substantially delivered.

## The Hydrocarbon Vision 2030 architecture

The 18 August commissioning fits within a substantial policy trajectory that the Government of India has progressively built across the past decade. The Hydrocarbon Vision 2030 for North-East India, released by the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas in 2016, established the strategic anchor: leverage the northeast's substantial hydrocarbon potential (the region contains some of India's most substantial onshore oil and gas reserves in the Upper Assam Shelf and adjacent geological formations), enhance regional access to clean fuels, and accelerate the wider economic development of the northeast through energy infrastructure investment.

The Vision framework has progressively been operationalised through multiple parallel initiatives. The Pradhan Mantri Urja Ganga project — the 2,540 kilometre Jagdishpur-Haldia-Bokaro-Dhamra natural gas pipeline launched from Varanasi by PM Modi in October 2016 — created the arterial trunk pipeline that would eventually connect eastern India to the wider national gas grid. Total estimated cost of Urja Ganga: approximately ₹51,000 crore. The Barauni-Guwahati Pipeline, awarded in 2018 at ₹1,100 crore for the 729 kilometre feeder segment, extended the Urja Ganga architecture toward the northeast. The North East Gas Grid, executed by IGGL, provides the downstream distribution architecture within the northeast itself.

Layered onto this pipeline infrastructure is the wider PNG (Piped Natural Gas) and CNG (Compressed Natural Gas) distribution architecture that City Gas Distribution licensees are progressively building in individual urban centres. Progressive urban gas distribution licensing rounds through the Petroleum and Natural Gas Regulatory Board (PNGRB) have covered progressively more northeast cities over recent years.

The cumulative effect: an architecture that a decade ago barely existed in the northeast is now progressively taking operational shape. The 18 August Nagaland connection is one substantial milestone within that broader trajectory.

## What comes next

For the NEGG itself, the immediate priority is progressive completion of the remaining state connections. ONGC is developing hook-up facilities at Jantapathar and Kasomarigaon to connect additional Jorhat Asset producing fields with the NEGG — progressively increasing the volume of gas available for downstream distribution. Additional branch pipelines toward Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya, Sikkim and Arunachal Pradesh remain under progressive construction.

For Nagaland specifically, the next phase is progressive expansion of city gas distribution within Dimapur (and eventually Kohima), progressive addition of CNG dispensing infrastructure for transport, and progressive addition of industrial customers whose new investment decisions can now be built around reliable natural gas supply.

For the wider northeast, the substantial promise of the Hydrocarbon Vision 2030 framework — a fully gas-connected northeast that participates fully in India's clean-energy transition and industrial expansion — remains progressively under construction but progressively closer to substantial realisation than at any point in the region's post-Independence trajectory.

## What it signifies

For Nagaland, the 18 August commissioning provides substantial evidence that the state is progressively being integrated into India's national infrastructure architecture on the same basis as any other Indian state. This is a substantial reversal of decades of structural marginalisation and one of the most consequential development-policy milestones the state has recorded in recent years.

For India's clean energy trajectory, the pipeline's substitution of LNG imports and reduction of gas flaring provides a substantial contribution to the country's wider emissions-reduction commitments. Alongside the 300.50 GW non-fossil electricity capacity milestone documented in Bharat Samvad's 19 August Page 1 coverage, and the wider policy architecture across renewable energy, natural gas expansion, and energy efficiency, this substantial contribution progressively strengthens India's overall trajectory toward the 2070 Net Zero commitment.

For India's fossil fuel import dependency, the 1.10 lakh SCM per day of substituted LNG imports adds to the accumulating record of domestic-production expansion that progressively reduces the country's exposure to global energy-market volatility. India remains one of the world's largest LNG importers; every SCM of domestic gas that reaches domestic markets is one SCM less that needs to be imported.

For the Aatmanirbhar Bharat framework, the 18 August commissioning demonstrates the substantial infrastructure-execution capacity of India's central public sector enterprises when they operate through purpose-built joint venture architectures. IGGL — five CPSEs assembled under a single-purpose vehicle for the NEGG — is an institutional template that other complex infrastructure programmes across India have progressively begun to adopt.

For India's northeast development trajectory, the Nagaland connection is one substantial milestone within the wider transformation that the region has progressively been undergoing across the past decade. Progressive road connectivity, air connectivity, digital connectivity, industrial investment, and now natural gas connectivity — the northeast is progressively becoming what PM Modi has described as India's substantial growth engine.

For now, on the morning of 20 August 2026, what stands on the institutional record is this: Nagaland has been formally integrated into the National Gas Grid for the first time in its history; the Dergaon-Dimapur Pipeline commissioning enables approximately 1.10 lakh SCM per day of natural gas from ONGC's Jorhat Asset to reach commercial markets; the Khoraghat GGS-1 evacuation facility complements the pipeline commissioning; substantial gas flaring has been eliminated; equivalent LNG imports have been substituted; approximately ₹300 crore of annual economic value has been unlocked; and one substantial milestone in the ₹9,265 crore North East Gas Grid architecture has been progressively delivered. From stranded upstream production to substantial downstream monetisation — the northeast's gas future is progressively arriving.
