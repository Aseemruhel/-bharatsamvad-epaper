LEAD: The Union Cabinet approved Semicon 2.0 on 15 July 2026 with an outlay of Rs 1,27,500 crore — the next phase of India's ambition to become a trusted global semiconductor hub. Building on the operational foundation laid by Semicon 1.0, the second phase advances the country's semiconductor strategy through six strategic pillars: research and development, chip design, machines and materials, additional fabrication facilities, talent development, and further strengthening of the Assembly, Testing, Marking and Packaging (ATMP) / Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Testing (OSAT) industry. Together, Semicon 1.0 and 2.0 now anchor one of the most comprehensive national semiconductor programmes any large economy has launched in the current decade.

## The six pillars — what Semicon 2.0 covers

Semicon 2.0 progressively expands the semiconductor mission from a fabrication-first focus toward a full-stack ecosystem architecture. Research and development anchors long-term capability building across process nodes, materials science and device physics. Chip design brings Indian talent and firms into the design frontier for next-generation logic, memory, RF and specialised processors. Machines and materials — the historically overlooked upstream layer — brings semiconductor equipment, chemicals, gases and specialty materials manufacturing progressively onto Indian soil. Additional fabs expand the physical capacity to produce chips. Talent development strengthens the pipeline of engineers, researchers and technicians that every dimension of the ecosystem requires. Strengthening the ATMP/OSAT industry deepens India's position in advanced packaging — one of the most consequential single niches where domestic capability can compete at global tier.

## The scale — 12 projects, six states, Rs 1.64 lakh crore

The operational scale of the semiconductor mission is now substantial. Twelve projects have been approved across six states, mobilising approximately Rs 1.64 lakh crore in cumulative investments. Semicon 1.0's ₹76,000 crore outlay laid the foundation for the country's first fabrication facilities and packaging plants; Semicon 2.0's Rs 1,27,500 crore outlay progressively builds the next tier — research infrastructure, design capacity, equipment and materials manufacturing, and the wider talent architecture that anchors sustained competitiveness.

## Electronics production — the wider trajectory

The Semicon 2.0 announcement sits within a wider electronics manufacturing trajectory that has progressively been reshaped over the past decade. Electronics production in India has grown nearly seven-fold to Rs 13.11 lakh crore in 2025-26 — from a base that was substantially smaller a decade earlier. The semiconductor programme provides the upstream anchor for this wider trajectory: every domestically-fabricated chip, every locally-packaged device, every Indian-designed IC block progressively strengthens the value addition captured within India rather than being routed through offshore supply chains.

## What it signals

For India's electronics ecosystem, Semicon 2.0 progressively delivers the upstream capacity that domestic manufacturing has long lacked. For India's defence and strategic sectors, the domestic semiconductor architecture reduces dependency on external suppliers whose export controls can be weaponised in periods of strategic tension. For India's Viksit Bharat 2047 trajectory, the Rs 1,27,500 crore commitment provides operational evidence that the country is progressively building the technology-manufacturing base that developed-economy status requires. And for India's global standing, the emergence of a comprehensive semiconductor programme — spanning research, design, fabrication, materials, packaging and talent — progressively positions India alongside the small number of countries that possess genuine full-stack capacity in one of the most consequential single technology categories of the twenty-first century.

From Semicon 1.0 to Semicon 2.0. From fabrication-first to full-stack. From import dependence to comprehensive domestic ecosystem. India's semiconductor mission has entered its most operationally consequential phase.
