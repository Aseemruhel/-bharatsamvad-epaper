LEAD: In a substantive expression of what mainstream reintegration can operationally deliver for surrendered Maoist cadres, seven former insurgents from the Gadchiroli district of Maharashtra have cleared the Secondary School Certificate (SSC) examination in 2025 — the tenth-standard board examination that anchors the Indian school system. Three of the seven have secured first-class results. All seven had previously operated within the armed insurgent architecture of the Gadchiroli-Chhattisgarh border belt, carrying weapons, participating in cadre operations, and living the substantially isolated existence that Maoist recruitment progressively demands. Today, they carry SSC certificates instead. The journey from weapons to books, for these seven individuals, is complete.

## The individual journeys — from cadre to classroom

Each of the seven represents a personal decision — taken by the individual, at a specific point in their operational cadre life — to lay down arms, walk out of the forest, surrender to the authorities under Maharashtra's structured surrender-and-rehabilitation policy, and begin the substantially long process of rebuilding a civilian identity. For some, that decision followed the loss of comrades in encounters with security forces. For others, the influence of family members or spouses who had earlier defected. For still others, the progressive recognition that the movement itself had failed to deliver what it had originally promised.

Whatever the individual trigger, the operational architecture that received them was consistent — Maharashtra Police's surrender protocol, the state's rehabilitation and compensation framework, and the district-level education, skill-training and livelihood infrastructure progressively built across Gadchiroli under successive state governments.

## Why the SSC matters

The Secondary School Certificate is more than an educational qualification. In the Indian institutional context, the SSC is the operational threshold beyond which a range of formal opportunities becomes accessible — government employment applications, ITI enrolment, vocational training programmes, further school and college education, and formal-sector employment across manufacturing and services. For a former cadre whose operational identity was previously defined by weapon-carrying and forest-based insurgent activity, the SSC certificate progressively opens the door to every one of those pathways.

The three first-class results are additionally significant. First-class marks (typically 60 per cent or above in the Maharashtra board's grading architecture) demonstrate not merely completion but substantive academic capability — capability that can progressively support entry to higher education, competitive examinations, and professional employment tracks that would otherwise be closed.

## The wider Gadchiroli story

The 2025 SSC results do not sit in isolation. They form part of a substantively wider Gadchiroli story that has progressively taken shape over the recent operational cycle. In October 2025, one of the largest single Naxal surrenders in the district's history took place when 61 cadres — including Politburo and Central Committee member Mallojula Venugopal Rao alias Bhupathi — laid down arms before Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis. In September 2026, Bhupathi was progressively appointed Coordinator of the Ekal Programme at Gondwana University, tasked with informing tribal Gram Sabhas about their PESA and Forest Rights Act entitlements — one of the most consequential rehabilitation appointments in India's post-2014 counter-Naxal architecture.

Alongside these headline events, the substantive rehabilitation work has progressively continued at the individual level — through vocational training programmes, skill-development enrolments, small-business start-up support, agricultural livelihood assistance, and — as the SSC results demonstrate — education continuation for those cadres whose formal schooling had been interrupted by their entry into the armed movement. The seven who cleared SSC in 2025 represent one substantive layer of that operational continuum.

## Why education-based rehabilitation matters

Education is a substantively different rehabilitation tool from most alternatives. Financial compensation supports immediate needs but does not create long-term stability. Skill training provides livelihood options but does not necessarily expand the wider life-choices space. Employment placements deliver income but can be lost when circumstances change. Education, by contrast, is progressively cumulative and progressively unlocking — every subsequent qualification opens a wider set of opportunities than the last, and the certifications cannot be taken away.

For former cadres, education additionally serves a substantive identity-transformation function. The daily rhythm of attending classes, submitting assignments, sitting examinations and receiving results progressively rebuilds a civilian identity structure that armed cadre life had substantially eroded. It progressively re-establishes the individual's relationship with time, discipline, achievement and community — the same social architecture that civilian society is built on.

## What it signals

For Maharashtra's counter-Naxal architecture, the seven SSC certificates demonstrate that the substantive rehabilitation-and-reintegration component of the state's approach is progressively delivering measurable outcomes. For the wider Gadchiroli tribal population — including active Maoist cadres whose surrender decision is still pending — the visible success of the seven former cadres provides substantive evidence that mainstream reintegration is genuinely possible and genuinely rewarding. For India's national Naxal-elimination framework under Union Home Minister Amit Shah's 31 March 2026 target deadline, the Gadchiroli seven represent one substantive expression of what the wider surrender-and-rehabilitation policy can operationally produce at the individual level. And for India's development-led counter-insurgency doctrine, the education outcomes progressively validate the substantive proposition that the movement's ideological hold can most effectively be broken not through military force alone, but through the systematic provision of the education, livelihood and dignity opportunities the movement had originally claimed to fight for.

From weapons to books. From cadre life to classroom life. From armed identity to educational identity. Seven certificates from the 2025 SSC examination in Gadchiroli capture, at the individual level, what India's wider counter-Naxal rehabilitation architecture progressively aspires to deliver at scale.
