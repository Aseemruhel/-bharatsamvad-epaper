LEAD: India's national examination system, one of the most consequential institutional pillars supporting the higher-education aspirations of tens of millions of young Indians every year, is set for a comprehensive reset. On Sunday, 26 July 2026, Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced the constitution of a high-powered task force on National Testing Agency (NTA) examination reforms — a six-member expert panel to be chaired by Infosys co-founder and technology-governance veteran Nandan Nilekani, and mandated to recommend structural, technological, and institutional reforms across the ecosystem that administers examinations affecting more than a crore students annually. The announcement, delivered through a video message posted on Sunday evening, sits within a three-pronged government response to the crisis that unfolded across the country over the past several weeks: the task force for long-term structural reform; the Public Examination Amendment Bill to be moved in Parliament today for stronger anti-leak provisions; and fast-track courts already established to expedite the prosecution of accused persons already in judicial custody.

The Prime Minister's announcement follows one of the most consequential institutional moments of the current session: the resignation of Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan on Saturday, 25 July, following the conclusion of the 36-day agitation led by the CJP student coalition (whose peaceful winding-down Bharat Samvad had appealed for on Friday's Page 1). The trigger for the wider crisis was the alleged NEET-UG 2026 paper leak in May, which sparked mass student protests across Delhi, Patna, Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Kolkata, and other cities, and progressively drew in civil society, political parties across the spectrum, and the wider examination-taking public into the substantive demand for meaningful institutional accountability. The Government's Sunday announcement is the operational institutional response.

## The six-member task force

The composition of the panel signals the Government's substantive intent to draw expertise from the specific domains that the NTA reform challenge requires. Each member brings a distinct institutional experience that maps to a dimension of the reform mandate.

**Nandan Nilekani** — chair of the task force, co-founder of Infosys, and one of India's most consequential technology-governance figures over the past two decades. Nilekani chaired the Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI) that built the Aadhaar system — arguably the largest identity-verification infrastructure ever deployed globally. He was subsequently a co-architect of the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) that transformed India's payments landscape. His combination of private-sector technology leadership at Infosys (CEO 2002-2007; Non-Executive Chairman since 2017), successful public-sector governance at UIDAI, and multi-government appointment record across political dispensations makes him uniquely positioned to lead a task force whose credibility must span political cycles.

**Dr S Somanath** — former Chairman of the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), under whose leadership India achieved the Chandrayaan-3 lunar south pole soft landing in August 2023, one of the most consequential achievements in the country's space history. Dr Somanath brings the institutional depth of running a large, security-sensitive, high-stakes public-sector organisation with substantial technology architecture — exactly the profile the NTA will need to emulate.

**Tapan Deka** — former Director of the Intelligence Bureau (IB), bringing decades of security-intelligence experience directly relevant to the paper-leak, cyber-security, and network-of-conspirators dimensions of the crisis. Effective examination security requires not only technology but the operational security mindset that intelligence services embody. Deka's inclusion signals that the reform will substantively address the security architecture, not merely the technology stack.

**Prof V Kamakoti** — Director of IIT Madras, one of India's most consequential technology institutions and a substantial centre of research in cyber-security, artificial intelligence, and digital-infrastructure engineering. Kamakoti brings both institutional-academic weight and hands-on technology-research credibility. IIT Madras itself has been progressively involved in the design of India's digital public infrastructure.

**Anita Karwal** — former Education Secretary in the Government of India, and among the most experienced Indian administrators on school and higher education governance. Karwal brings the specific institutional and administrative knowledge of how the education-ministry-NTA-state-university ecosystem functions in operational reality — a substantive complement to the technology-driven perspectives that the other members bring.

**Amrit Lal Meena** — a logistics expert and senior IAS officer whose track record includes complex, large-scale administrative delivery. The NTA's operational challenge is fundamentally a logistical one — administering examinations at more than 4,000 centres across the country, on the same day, in synchronised windows, with millions of candidates and physical question papers. Meena's inclusion signals that the task force will address the logistical dimension with the seriousness it deserves.

## The mandate

The task force has been asked to submit its recommendations across four principal dimensions.

**Examination process reform**: The panel will conduct an end-to-end analysis of the current examination process — from question-paper preparation through storage, distribution, examination-day administration, evaluation, and result declaration. Recommendations are expected to substantially reduce the number of vulnerability points at which paper leaks or manipulation can occur.

**Data security architecture**: NTA's information-security protocols — both for the digital question banks that increasingly underpin computer-based tests and for the physical paper-based examinations that remain the mainstay for many national tests — will be reviewed against contemporary threat models. Recommendations are expected to bring NTA's cyber-security posture in line with what any critical-infrastructure operator would maintain.

**Structural and organisational reform**: The NTA's own institutional structure — its staffing, its board, its accountability mechanisms, its relationship with the Ministry of Education and with the states — will be reviewed. This addresses the substantive concern that the NTA's operational capacity has not scaled with the enormous expansion of the examinations it conducts.

**Technology-driven examinations**: The panel is specifically asked to recommend measures for maximum use of technology in the examination ecosystem. Options being considered by observers include on-demand examinations across the year rather than single high-stakes windows, computer-based tests in place of paper-based ones where feasible, secure biometric authentication of candidates, real-time monitoring, and adaptive question-selection.

The task force is expected to submit its recommendations within a defined timeline that the Government will confirm in Parliament today. The Radhakrishnan Committee, constituted in 2024 following the NEET-UG 2024 paper leak, had submitted its recommendations within two months; the Nilekani task force is expected to operate on a similar or accelerated timeline.

## The three-pronged response

The Nilekani task force is one leg of a substantially wider institutional response that the Government has calibrated across three distinct dimensions.

**The judicial pillar** — fast-track courts have already been established for the pending paper-leak cases. The Prime Minister's Sunday message specifically noted that "those who played with the future of students are now rotting in jails" — a reference to the CBI's ongoing prosecution of the accused in the NEET-UG 2026 case, alongside earlier prosecutions from the 2024 crisis. The fast-track courts provide expedited judicial processing of these cases, sending an operational message about the consequences of paper-leak participation.

**The legislative pillar** — the Public Examination Amendment Bill will be moved in Parliament today, 27 July 2026, with stringent additional provisions building on the Public Examination (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act enacted in 2024 following the earlier crisis. The 2024 Act was itself one of the most substantial legislative interventions in Indian examination law — providing for imprisonment of up to ten years, fines up to ₹1 crore, and asset-attachment provisions for those convicted of examination malpractice. The amendment bill will strengthen these provisions further.

**The structural pillar** — the Nilekani task force itself, whose recommendations will progressively shape the institutional architecture of the NTA and the wider examination ecosystem for years to come.

The three-pillar architecture is substantively significant. Judicial and legislative measures address the individuals and networks that conduct paper leaks; the task force addresses the institutional structure that permitted those leaks. Both dimensions are necessary; neither alone is sufficient. The Government's response combines both.

## The Nilekani track record

The choice of Nandan Nilekani to chair the task force is a substantive institutional signal in itself. Nilekani has, across a two-decade public-service arc under successive governments of different political dispensations, established one of the most respected profiles in Indian technology-governance.

At the UIDAI (Chairman, 2009-2014), Nilekani led the design and initial rollout of Aadhaar — the biometric identity system that now covers virtually the entire Indian population and provides the digital-identity backbone for direct benefit transfers, subsidy delivery, banking access, and dozens of other services. The UIDAI was, at the time of its establishment, one of the most ambitious identity-infrastructure projects ever attempted globally; its success in delivering unique digital identities to over a billion residents changed India's public-service delivery architecture.

As co-architect of the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) — India's real-time payments system that now handles more monthly transactions than any comparable system globally — Nilekani was instrumental in designing what has become one of the most consequential Indian innovations in digital public infrastructure. UPI's design principles — open architecture, interoperability, low friction, high volume — provide the template for exactly the kind of examination-technology architecture that the NTA reform will require.

At Infosys, Nilekani's tenure as CEO (2002-2007) coincided with the Indian IT industry's most consequential period of global expansion. His return as Non-Executive Chairman in 2017 marks a substantive continuity in one of India's most consequential corporate governance stories.

His combination of private-sector technology leadership, successful public-sector governance, and multi-government appointment record — including chairing task forces under both UPA and NDA governments — makes him uniquely positioned to lead a reform effort whose credibility must span political cycles and win the confidence of students, families, states, and institutions across the political spectrum.

## The 2024 precedent — and what has changed

This is not the first high-level committee constituted to reform the NTA. In June 2024, following the NEET-UG 2024 paper leak, the Ministry of Education constituted a seven-member committee chaired by Dr K Radhakrishnan (former ISRO Chairman and current Chairman of the Board of Governors at IIT Kanpur). That committee submitted its recommendations within two months and led to several substantial reforms — including the Public Examination (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act 2024, the restructuring of the NTA that Dharmendra Pradhan had announced for 2025 (including the withdrawal of NTA from recruitment examinations to focus on higher education entrance tests), and the creation of ten additional posts within the NTA structure.

The 2026 crisis, however, has made clear that those earlier reforms — substantial as they were — did not go far enough. The NEET-UG 2026 paper leak, occurring less than two years after the 2024 reform architecture was put in place, demonstrated that the underlying vulnerabilities had not been fully addressed. The Nilekani task force operates against this backdrop. Its recommendations will need to substantively exceed those of the 2024 committee — not merely repeating the earlier reform template, but designing an examination architecture that is genuinely resilient against the network of malpractice-actors that has proven persistent.

The specific inclusion of Tapan Deka, former IB Director, on the Nilekani panel — a member with no equivalent on the Radhakrishnan committee — signals that the security-intelligence dimension of the reform will be substantively strengthened this time.

## What comes next

In the immediate horizon, the Public Examination Amendment Bill will be moved in Parliament today, with the Government indicating that stringent additional provisions will be introduced. The task force is expected to hold its first sitting within days of the Prime Minister's announcement, and to progressively engage stakeholders across the examination ecosystem — students, states, universities, coaching-industry actors, and civil-society representatives — as it develops its recommendations.

In the medium term, the task force's report will progressively be released in stages, with the Government committing to accelerated implementation. Structural changes to the NTA — including any statutory-status modifications, board-composition changes, or accountability-mechanism strengthening — will require additional legislative action.

In the longer term, the substantive combination of reformed NTA, stronger legislative deterrent, expedited judicial processing, and technology-driven examination architecture will progressively rebuild the trust that has been damaged over the past decade of episodic paper-leak crises.

## What it signifies

For India's students and their families, the announcement offers substantive institutional assurance that the crisis of the past several weeks is being met with a proportionate government response — not a defensive one, but a forward-looking institutional reset.

For the CJP-led movement that Bharat Samvad appealed to for peaceful conclusion on Friday's Page 1, the announcement provides the substantive institutional consequence that peaceful democratic protest can achieve. The movement's disciplined pressure, combined with ministerial accountability (Pradhan's resignation) and executive action (the task force), demonstrates that constitutional democracy remains capable of institutional self-correction.

For India's wider governance record, the Nilekani task force adds another instance to the Government's demonstrated readiness to bring the country's most credible technology-governance figures into critical institutional reform. From UIDAI to UPI to the NTA reform, the pattern of drawing on Nilekani's expertise across multiple governments and political dispensations is one of the most consequential continuities in India's institutional development over the past two decades.

For now, on the morning of 27 July 2026, what stands on the institutional record is this: a six-member expert panel of exceptional caliber has been constituted; the Government's three-pronged response combines structural reform, legislative deterrence, and judicial acceleration; the wider CJP-led agitation has ended with the substantive victory of institutional accountability; and India's examination ecosystem — the gateway to higher education, professional opportunity, and social mobility for an entire generation of young Indians — is being systematically reset. The reset has begun. Its credibility will be measured in how completely the reforms are implemented over the coming months and years.
