LEAD: India's national sports architecture has, on the strength of one of the most consequential Union Cabinet approvals in the country's post-Independence sports history, been given the institutional and financial foundation it has waited for. The revamped Khelo India Scheme, announced by Union Minister for Youth Affairs and Sports Dr Mansukh Mandaviya following approval by the Cabinet chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, provides a combined outlay of ₹36,441 crore for the period 2026-27 to 2030-31 — the largest sports-development commitment India has ever made. The allocation splits into ₹29,054 crore for the Khelo India Scheme itself and ₹7,387 crore for the enhanced Assistance to National Sports Federations (ANSF) programme. Compared to the previous cycle's Khelo India allocation of ₹3,790 crore for 2021-26, the new outlay represents nearly an eight-fold expansion. Compared to the number of athletes supported under Khelo India — 2,745 at the time of the current scheme's conclusion — the new architecture will progressively expand the supported athlete pool to approximately 27,000, a nearly ten-fold expansion. And compared to the ambition of the previous Khelo India generation, which produced measurable improvements in India's global sports positioning across the 2018 Youth Olympics through 2024 Paris Games arc, the new scheme sets substantively higher targets: Top 10 at the 2036 Olympics, Top 5 by 2047.

This is not merely a budget expansion. It is a systemic redesign of the way India identifies, develops, supports, and progressively delivers world-class athletes across an ecosystem stretching from village playgrounds to the Olympic podium. It aligns explicitly with the twin institutional frameworks of Khelo Bharat Niti 2025 (the recently promulgated National Sports Policy) and the National Education Policy 2020, both of which recognise sport as a substantive dimension of national development rather than as an ancillary activity. It anchors within the wider vision of Viksit Bharat@2047 — the developmental horizon that the Government has progressively made the organising framework for cross-sectoral policy design. And it positions the country for the substantive institutional lift required to bid successfully for the 2036 Olympic and Paralympic Games and the 2030 Commonwealth Games, both of which India is progressively preparing candidatures for.

## The numbers that redefine the ambition

The financial scale of the new commitment is worth being clear-eyed about. The combined ₹36,441 crore outlay works out to approximately ₹7,288 crore per year across the five-year cycle. This represents a sports investment intensity that India has never previously approached. To put the numbers in comparative context: the entire Khelo India budget for the previous five-year cycle (₹3,790 crore) was less than one-quarter of what will be spent on Khelo India alone in each single year of the new cycle.

The state and central share pattern is also substantively revised. Of the ₹29,054 crore Khelo India Scheme allocation, ₹28,489 crore will come from the central government's share, with ₹565 crore contributed by states as their share of future sports infrastructure development projects. This reflects the Government's willingness to substantially take on the funding load while progressively encouraging state-level co-investment.

The athlete expansion — from 2,745 to approximately 27,000 supported athletes — carries substantively deeper significance than the raw multiple suggests. Under the previous architecture, Khelo India Athletes received support through coaching at National Centres of Excellence under the Sports Authority of India, equipment provision, medical care, and a monthly Out of Pocket Allowance (OPA). Extending this level of institutional support to nearly 27,000 athletes represents a categorical shift in the scale at which India will identify and nurture sporting talent. It is one of the most substantial expansions of the country's talent-development apparatus that any sector has produced in recent years.

## The new institutional architecture

The revamped scheme introduces several new institutional layers that together represent the substantive design innovation of the 2026-31 cycle.

**Khelo India Feeder Schools (KIFS)** — a new institutional tier designed to integrate sports systematically into school education, providing early-stage talent identification and structured training pathways for students demonstrating athletic potential. The KIFS network will progressively cover school districts across the country, ensuring that the substantial talent that has historically been lost between grassroots and elite levels is progressively retained and nurtured.

**Khelo India Utkrishta Vidyalayas (KIUV)** — schools of excellence that will serve as the intermediate institutional layer between KIFS and elite training centres. Utkrishta Vidyalayas will provide the substantively enhanced coaching, sports science support, academic integration, and residential infrastructure required to progressively convert identified potential into competition-ready capability.

**Emerging Khelo India Athletes (E-KIA)** — a new category of supported athletes, distinct from the elite Khelo India Athletes designation, that will progressively expand the supported cohort to the 27,000-athlete target. E-KIA participants will receive structured institutional support at a stage of development earlier than the current elite designation permits, addressing the substantial dropout that has historically occurred between promising district-level performance and elite selection.

**National Coach Accreditation Board (NCAB)** — a new institutional apparatus designed to standardise coaching qualifications across India, progressively improve the quality of coaching available to athletes at every level, and address one of the most substantially recognised bottlenecks in Indian sports development. Coaching quality has consistently been identified by Indian athletes and analysts as a critical constraint; NCAB is designed to progressively resolve it.

**National Sports Repository System** — a data infrastructure supporting evidence-based planning, athlete management, and performance analytics across the entire Khelo India ecosystem. This substantively institutionalises the sports-science and technology dimension that the scheme's stated framework specifically identifies.

**Olympic Training Centres** — the substantively expanded network of high-performance training facilities that will progressively serve as the operational base for elite athletes preparing for international competition. Rural sports infrastructure will also be substantially expanded to broaden the base from which talent progressively emerges.

## The Olympic roadmap: 2036 and 2047

The stated targets — Top 10 at the 2036 Olympics, Top 5 by 2047 — reflect one of the most ambitious sports-diplomacy trajectories any large developing economy has publicly committed to. At the Paris 2024 Olympics, India finished 71st in the medal table with 6 medals (0 gold, 1 silver, 5 bronze). Moving from that positioning to a Top 10 finish within a decade requires a step-change that only substantive institutional and financial investment can produce. The new Khelo India Scheme provides that substantive institutional and financial investment.

The 2036 timeline is also aligned with India's active bid to host the 2036 Olympic and Paralympic Games. Ahmedabad has been identified as the substantive candidate city, with substantial infrastructure investment already underway to progressively meet the operational requirements of an Olympic hosting bid. The International Olympic Committee's decision on the 2036 host is expected in the coming years; hosting the Games would provide India with the substantial home-advantage that host nations typically enjoy in medal-table performance. The 2030 Commonwealth Games hosting bid, similarly, is progressing on parallel tracks.

The 2047 Top 5 ambition maps directly to the wider Viksit Bharat @ 2047 vision. Sports has been recognised, in the substantive institutional framework the Government has progressively built, as one of the dimensions of national development that a Viksit Bharat should demonstrate. The physical, mental, cultural, and identity dimensions of sport progressively support the wider goal of comprehensive national development that Viksit Bharat envisages.

## The Aatmanirbhar Bharat and Global South dimensions

Alongside the scheme's substantial financial commitment, the revamped architecture carries substantive strategic dimensions that connect to India's wider foreign-policy and development-diplomacy priorities.

**Aatmanirbhar Bharat in sports.** The scheme's focus on indigenous coach development through NCAB, indigenous sports-science infrastructure through the National Sports Repository System, and support for indigenous Indian sports (kabaddi, kho-kho, mallakhamba, gatka, and others) that the Khelo India Games have progressively featured, positions the scheme within the wider self-reliance framework. India will progressively reduce its dependency on foreign coaching expertise for certain sports categories, though foreign expertise will substantially continue where it adds specific value (as the ANSF architecture explicitly provides for).

**Global South sports cooperation.** The ANSF architecture specifically provides for sports cooperation with Global South countries — a substantive dimension of India's soft-power engagement with developing economies across Africa, Southeast Asia, and Latin America. Indian sports institutions will progressively engage with counterpart institutions in partner countries, share training methodologies, host visiting athletes and coaches, and progressively build the sports-development dimension of India's substantive Global South leadership.

**Indigenous sports on the international stage.** As Indian sports infrastructure progressively matures, the substantial international profile of indigenous Indian sports — kabaddi at the international kabaddi championships, kho-kho at newly established international competition circuits, and yoga (progressively recognised in international sporting contexts) — will progressively expand.

## Integration with NEP 2020 and Khelo Bharat Niti 2025

The revamped scheme does not exist in institutional isolation. It sits within a substantively coordinated policy architecture that has been progressively built across the past decade.

**Khelo Bharat Niti 2025** — India's newly promulgated National Sports Policy — provides the strategic doctrine within which the Khelo India Scheme operates. The Niti recognises sport as a dimension of national development spanning grassroots participation, elite performance, professional leagues, sports-goods manufacturing, sports-medicine research, and international sports diplomacy. The revamped Khelo India Scheme is the operational instrument through which several of the Niti's stated objectives will progressively be delivered.

**National Education Policy 2020** — the substantive educational reform that has progressively redefined India's school and higher-education architecture — explicitly identifies physical education, sports, and yoga as integral to comprehensive student development rather than as extra-curricular add-ons. Khelo India Feeder Schools and Utkrishta Vidyalayas operationalise this integration by embedding structured sports training within the school architecture that NEP 2020 has mandated.

**Fit India Movement** — launched in 2019 to encourage widespread physical fitness — will progressively expand under the revamped scheme, complemented by the Sundays on Cycle campaign that has progressively grown into one of the largest recurring civic-fitness initiatives in the country.

Together, this policy architecture describes an approach in which sport is not merely elite competition preparation but a comprehensive national capability spanning fitness, health, participation, competition, industry, diplomacy, and identity.

## What comes next

In the immediate horizon, the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports will progressively operationalise the new architecture: NCAB is expected to be constituted and progressively begin its coach accreditation work; the E-KIA category will be progressively populated as talent identification progresses; KIFS and KIUV will progressively be established across the country; and the National Sports Repository System will progressively be built.

In the medium term, the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics will provide the first substantive test of whether the new architecture is progressively producing measurable improvements. Elite athletes currently supported under TOPS and adjacent programmes will progressively compete at LA 2028, and the wider athlete pool being progressively supported through the new architecture will begin producing substantive results in the 2030-2036 cycle leading to the Los Angeles Games and beyond.

In the longer term, the 2036 Olympics will provide the substantive test of whether the Top 10 target has been delivered. Whether India hosts these Games or competes as a visiting nation, the 2036 performance will progressively define whether the current investment has produced the substantive transformation that its architects have committed to.

## What it signifies

For **India's young athletes**, the revamped scheme offers substantive institutional assurance that the country is committed to progressively supporting them at scale. From the village playground to the Olympic podium, the pathway is progressively being institutionalised. The days in which promising talent was lost between grassroots identification and elite selection are progressively ending.

For **India's sports federations**, the enhanced ANSF architecture provides substantial additional resources across coaching, sports science, equipment, international exposure, and foreign expertise. Federations have historically been under-resourced in the Indian institutional structure; the ANSF component progressively addresses this.

For **India's international sports diplomacy**, the substantive institutional commitment provides the foundation on which credible bids for the 2030 Commonwealth Games and 2036 Olympic and Paralympic Games can be built. Host-nation credibility rests on demonstrated institutional capability, and the new Khelo India architecture provides substantive evidence of that capability.

For **India's wider developmental narrative**, the scheme adds a substantial dimension to the Viksit Bharat @ 2047 framework. A developed nation demonstrates its development across multiple dimensions; sports is one of the most publicly visible of these. The substantive commitment to Top 5 by 2047 makes sports one of the substantive institutional pillars supporting the wider developmental narrative.
