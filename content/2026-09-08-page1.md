LEAD: BRICS is a major grouping of emerging economies that promotes cooperation in economic development, trade, finance and global governance. Originally comprising Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa, the grouping has expanded in recent years, increasing its geopolitical and economic significance. As the world order progressively shifts from a unipolar to a multipolar architecture, BRICS is increasingly seen as the platform through which the Global South articulates its collective voice.

## The four-area agenda

BRICS' agenda focuses on four broad areas: peace and security, economic and financial cooperation, sustainable development, and people-to-people exchanges. Each area provides the operational architecture through which the grouping's members progressively coordinate on shared priorities — from the New Development Bank and Contingent Reserve Arrangement in financial cooperation, to climate and clean-energy coordination in sustainable development, to educational and cultural exchanges in the people-to-people track.

## Why BRICS matters for the Global South

The article highlights BRICS' potential to strengthen multipolarity and South-South cooperation. In a global institutional environment where post-1945 architectures — the UN Security Council, the IMF, the World Bank — continue to reflect a distribution of power that no longer matches contemporary economic and demographic realities, BRICS provides one of the substantive alternative platforms through which emerging economies coordinate positions, share resources, and progressively assert their collective interests.

## What BRICS means for India

For India, BRICS is an important platform to advocate reform of global institutions, inclusive development and a stronger voice for developing countries, while navigating differences among major members. India's approach has progressively focused on delivery-oriented multilateralism — using the platform to advance concrete cooperation on health, digital public infrastructure, agriculture, climate action and technology, while simultaneously working through bilateral and multilateral tracks to shape the wider global order.
