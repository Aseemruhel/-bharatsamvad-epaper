LEAD: India is transforming its border villages into vibrant centres of growth, opportunity and security through one of the most operationally consequential single border-development programmes the country has launched in recent years. The Vibrant Villages Programme (VVP), launched in 2023, focuses on the comprehensive development of selected villages along India's international land borders. Together, VVP-I and VVP-II now cover 2,616-plus villages with a combined outlay of ₹11,639 crore. On 2 April 2025, the Union Cabinet approved VVP-II — extending the model to all land borders under central funding, implemented by the Ministry of Home Affairs.

## VVP-I — the operational track record

Under VVP-I, 111 road projects worth ₹2,481.93 crore have been sanctioned, connecting 134 previously unconnected villages to the mainland road network. 327 tourism projects worth ₹145 crore have been approved, opening border villages as destinations rather than dead-ends. By July 2026, more than 8,800 activities had been conducted — health camps, awareness drives, service delivery camps, and skill training — bringing government schemes and civic services to communities that had historically been at the margins of state reach.

## VVP-II — extending the model to all land borders

The Union Cabinet's 2 April 2025 approval of VVP-II extends the operational model from the initial northern-border focus to all of India's international land borders. Unlike the first phase, VVP-II is funded entirely by the Centre and implemented directly by the Ministry of Home Affairs. The programme covers villages along India's borders with Bangladesh, Bhutan, Myanmar, Nepal, and additional stretches not covered under VVP-I, ensuring uniform developmental attention across the country's border-village architecture.

## Why the programme matters

For decades, India's border villages had progressively been emptying as residents migrated inward in search of better opportunities. An empty border is a strategic vulnerability — populated borders, by contrast, provide living evidence of sovereignty and a functional civilian ecosystem that supports the wider security architecture. By providing frontier citizens with infrastructure, awareness and economic power, the VVP fundamentally shifts India's border-development posture from marginal reach to comprehensive presence. The transformation from "last villages" to "first villages" — a paradigm the Government has progressively articulated — captures the operational logic underpinning both VVP-I and VVP-II.

The 111 road projects worth ₹2,481.93 crore that have connected 134 previously unconnected villages represent one operational dimension of this shift. The 327 tourism projects worth ₹145 crore represent another — border villages as economic destinations rather than as peripheries. The more than 8,800 health camps, awareness drives, service delivery camps and skill training activities conducted by July 2026 represent the softer but equally consequential dimension of state presence at the frontier.

With VVP-II now extending the model across every land border under central funding and Ministry of Home Affairs implementation, the operational architecture through which India's frontier villages progressively transform into vibrant, secure, connected communities has been formally scaled up.
