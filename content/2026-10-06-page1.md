LEAD: Finland has formally added its voice to the group of countries publicly backing India's bid for a permanent seat on the United Nations Security Council. In his first media briefing since taking up his post, Finland's Ambassador to India Mikko Kivikoski on Monday, 5 October 2026, explicitly stated Helsinki's position: Finland supports India's permanency in the Security Council, and additionally supports a wider reform that would add two new permanent members from Asia, two from Africa and one from Latin America. Alongside the UNSC endorsement, Kivikoski announced an ambitious bilateral trade objective — doubling trade between the two countries from the current level of about $3 billion, with the India-European Union Free Trade Agreement positioned as the main operational driver.

## The UNSC endorsement — Finland joins a growing group

Finland's explicit support for India's permanent UNSC seat adds the country to a progressively lengthening list of states publicly backing the Indian bid. Russia, through Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov's remarks at the UN General Assembly, has recently reaffirmed its support. Senior American economist Jeffrey Sachs has publicly called for both the Indian bid and for Chinese backing. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres has endorsed broader Security Council reform at the recent BRICS Summit. The Finnish endorsement adds a Nordic European Union member to this architecture — one with a specific operational position on the shape of the reform itself.

Kivikoski framed Finland's position within the country's broader commitment to multilateralism, calling the reasoning "quite clear". The envoy went further than a simple endorsement of India, articulating Finland's view on the wider reform package: not just India's inclusion, but a substantively expanded permanent membership that would progressively rebalance the Council's regional representation — two new permanent members from Asia, two from Africa and one from Latin America. This is a reform architecture that would address the historical under-representation of entire continents on the current Council.

## The $3 billion trade — targeted to double

Bilateral trade between India and Finland currently stands at approximately $3 billion annually. The target articulated by Kivikoski — building on earlier public commitments by leaders of both countries — is to double this figure, with the India-EU Free Trade Agreement serving as the operational framework.

Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo, during his February visit to New Delhi, had previously stated that Finland aimed to double trade with India by the early 2030s. Kivikoski's remarks on Monday reaffirmed that timeline and connected it directly to the FTA architecture now progressively moving toward implementation. The envoy described the current year as one of "record engagements" between the two countries, citing Prime Minister Narendra Modi's exchanges with Finland, the state visit to India by Finnish President Alexander Stubb, and the Nordic-India Summit held in Oslo.

Finnish companies, Kivikoski said, are showing genuine interest in the Indian market. The sectoral dimensions in which Finnish industry has historically possessed depth — telecommunications, information technology, industrial automation, forestry and paper, maritime technology, clean energy, and defence electronics — align with multiple categories of Indian demand under the country's current industrial modernisation.

## The EU-India FTA — the operational enabler

Underlying Finland's trade-expansion target is the India-European Union Free Trade Agreement, whose negotiations were concluded on 27 January 2026 after years of negotiation across multiple rounds. The agreement is still awaiting signature and ratification by all 27 EU member states before it enters full operational force. Antti Herlevi, Trade Counsellor at the Finnish Embassy in New Delhi, had earlier in August indicated an expected finalisation timeline of late 2026 or early 2027.

Once operational, the FTA will progressively eliminate tariffs across a wide range of goods and services between India and the European Union, provide protections for investment flows, open government procurement opportunities in both directions, and establish rules for digital trade, intellectual property and sustainable development. For Finland specifically — a smaller EU economy with strong technology, engineering and clean-industry capabilities — the FTA opens access to one of the largest growth markets in the world without the tariff friction that currently constrains bilateral commercial activity.

## The Ukraine dimension

Alongside the UNSC and trade announcements, Finland used Monday's briefing to endorse India's longstanding position on the Russia-Ukraine conflict — that a negotiated dialogue is the only viable pathway to resolution. Kivikoski said directly that "a negotiated solution" is required, and that Finland "highly appreciates all the activities that India is conducting at the moment".

The Finnish endorsement of India's dialogue-based approach carries additional weight given Finland's own strategic situation. The country shares a 1,340-kilometre land border with Russia, joined NATO in 2023 following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, and has first-hand security exposure to the ongoing conflict. For a frontline NATO member with direct Russian border exposure to publicly align with India's negotiated-settlement approach represents a meaningful diplomatic endorsement of the Indian position.

## The 30,000-strong Indian community in Finland

Finland hosts over 30,000 permanent Indian residents, most of them working in the information and communication technology and engineering sectors. Kivikoski described the community as well-integrated and well-respected, and said Helsinki hopes to progressively welcome more skilled Indian professionals under the mobility partnership that the two countries have agreed to.

Finland's economic structure — a high-technology, knowledge-intensive economy with ageing demographics and acute shortages in specific skilled-labour categories — creates sustained demand for the kind of ICT and engineering talent that Indian professionals have historically supplied. The mobility partnership formalises what has in effect been an organic migration pattern over recent decades, and progressively opens the framework for its expansion.

## What it signals

For India's wider UNSC campaign, Finland's endorsement adds another substantive voice from the European Union to the group of states publicly backing permanent Council membership — one with specific policy content on the shape of the reform. For India-EU economic engagement, Finland's trade-doubling target captures what the FTA architecture is operationally expected to enable across multiple bilateral relationships once the agreement is signed and ratified. For India's position on the Russia-Ukraine conflict, Finnish endorsement from a frontline NATO member with direct Russian border exposure provides diplomatic validation of the dialogue-based approach. For India's mobility diplomacy, the Finnish framework progressively joins the growing architecture of structured skilled-labour partnerships India has concluded with Germany, Japan, Australia and others — providing structured pathways for Indian talent to serve global skill shortages.

From Nordic bilateral engagement to EU-wide trade liberalisation. From $3 billion to a targeted $6 billion. From UN Security Council status quo to a reform position with explicit Indian inclusion. The 5 October briefing by Finland's Ambassador captures, in a single media engagement, the operational depth of what Finland-India relations have progressively become.
